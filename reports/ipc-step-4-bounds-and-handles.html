IPC Step 4: Bounds and Handles

Step 4 of the slice in ipc-design.md, for gaps B6 and B8a. A message is held to 16 KiB and a mailbox to 256 messages or 1 MiB. Larger data goes in a published artifact, its handle goes in message_agent's artifacts:, and the receiver reads it with read_artifact. list_artifacts shows only what the caller may read.

  • Every refusal, by every route, leaves the sender running and tells it what it can do, naming only tools it holds.
  • A person's text is neither refused nor lost. A full mailbox takes it anyway, and aos-tui turns a line over the cap into a handle.
  • A full mailbox no longer lands in one request. The contract's text would have made a process stuck for good on any window under 1 MiB; that was measured, and changed. So mail can be waiting when a model decides. The step says so, and a model that exits anyway leaves it unread.
  • Old records replay as before, gated by bus_bounds.

What this does not close: one body larger than the model's window still fails every request. Such a body can exist only from before the cap. A verifier, being a sibling, gets evidence only if it parks and its lead attaches a handle. Decision 10's one bus file is not built, and nothing bounds the number of mailboxes.

Can a call failing on size get out? Over the compaction trigger, yes: the next attempts compact (TestACallFailingOnSizeGetsOut: 42 calls, 10 failed, all drained, on a window 2,000 chars above the trigger). Under the trigger, no: nothing shrinks the request, it fails the same way each time, and the mail is kept (TestACallFailingBelowTheTriggerStays). A tool's output in that gap does the same, and a run's system prompt counts towards it, so the trigger has to fit the real window. What an operator sees then: a backend.retry event for each failure; when the attempts run out (12 by default, about 10 minutes of backoff), exit code 1, retryable, with a checkpoint, and the reason model endpoint failed 12 times in a row: and the endpoint's error. The mail stays. It can be Recovered, and fails the same way. The way out is a lower AOS_LLM_CONTEXT_WINDOW or AOS_COMPACT_RATIO, so that the trigger falls below the real window. The book session accepted this over the way out growth used to give by chance.

Why the default policy's limit falls below two 12 KiB bodies, and whether that is right. That policy is for an unknown window: 8,000 chars, down to 4,000, and half of that is 2,000 bytes. I still think it is right. Sent together, compaction archives the first before any model reads it, and the model has an archived unread: line and a read_memory to make, which takes the same step and leaves the mail unshown if it does not. A run with a known window takes 32 KiB. The two older tests still fail without NoBusBounds after these fixes, so they keep it.

  • The rule: one step takes on at most 32 KiB, one fewer than KeepMessages messages and half of compaction's target, and at least one message, and nothing new while the last batch has not reached a model. 32 KiB is less than a tool output because that window has 34 KiB above its trigger.
  • The cost is steps. The default budget of 24 does not cover 1 MiB. The process exits on its budget with the rest unread; with a restart policy the chain closes and its senders are told.
  • And what the model has seen when it decides. It may exit with mail waiting: it has been told, the exit stands, and the rest become dead letters. Under the default policy, mail over 2,000 bytes comes one message a step.
  • Framing adds to bodies: a body of newlines renders to about 3 times its bytes, and the batch counts bodies, so such mail can still be archived before a read (TestFramingCanStillArchiveMailUnread).
  • A higher ratio has less room above the trigger. Validate checks hysteresis, not this room, which was already true of tool output.

  • Reading one page of a 10 MiB artifact reads, hashes and decodes the whole payload: 31.5 ms over 25 trials. 160 pages took 14 s with the runtime's own work, against a model call each of seconds.
  • The number of mailboxes is not bounded, so neither is the bus: the table is a slope, not a limit. A workflow of a few agents stays in tens of milliseconds, and hundreds of busy agents would not.
  • Dead-letter files last as long as the state directory, each now at most about 16 KiB from new mail.
  • A refused big send still sits in the sender's history: B6 measured its checkpoint at 2.1 MB for 1 MiB and 21 MB for 10 MiB, since its turns hold what it wrote.

4. Differences From the Contract

Decisions 1, 7, 8 and 10 are changed in place, and each says so. The table is in ipc-design.md, "Implemented: Step 4".

The text saidBuilt
"publish it with publish_artifact and send the artifact_id"Names artifacts, and only tools held
"at most 1 MiB of unread mail can arrive in one step"Batches
The cap and bound on any sendThe operator: cap yes, bound no, conversion in aos-tui
"under the receiver's capabilities"read_artifact is always available; its rules are the gate
published:*published.*
Not thereMetadata["continues"] on a new root; refusals that may change do not count as repeats

Two older tests now set NoBusBounds, with what they expect unchanged: TestArchivedMailIsNotShownUntilRead and TestAdoptionRequiresIdenticalMessages need several messages in one step, which batches split. TestFramingCanStillArchiveMailUnread shows the bounded path to the same notice.

5. Not Covered

  • One body larger than the window does not drain.
  • A child that never parks has no route to a parent's artifact.
  • A chain nobody closes, with room, still gives no outcome.
  • Archived mail nobody reads holds its place, so that mailbox can stay full until its chain ends.
  • The sender's own turn holds what it wrote: publishing 10 MiB is 10 MiB of model output and 21 MB of checkpoint. publish_artifact has no path form.
  • Rights are not revoked. A chain keeps a handle it was given.
  • The bus's total size, and dead-letter files.
