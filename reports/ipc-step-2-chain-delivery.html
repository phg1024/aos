IPC Step 2: Chain Delivery

This is step 2 of the slice in ipc-design.md, for defects B8c and B11 and gap B2b. Mail and subscriptions now belong to a process chain, not to one process, so a restart no longer costs an agent its subscriptions, and a rollback no longer loses mail.

Result:

  • B2b, B8c and B11 now assert, and pass.
  • One existing test is flaky under load, before and after this step: section 4.
  • B1, B2a, B3 and B4 still hold: FIFO across Recover, and over 25 trials of 4 senders × 50 messages, 0 lost, 0 duplicated, 0 corrupted, 0 out of order.
  • Senders in separate OS processes racing repeated Recovers lose nothing, in every trial of TestForwardingRacesRecover. That test cannot see a very narrow race on its own: section 4.
  • Eval's three ca78ad9 trials (31/31, 8/8, 9/9) and a v0.1.4 record (7/7) replay identically.

Still open: a sender is not told when its mail goes unread (B2c, B1c). Mail in an ended chain stays in the mailbox, with no event of its own, until step 3. Holding a message also makes this silence more common, because a send that used to be refused now succeeds.

Steps 3 to 5 are not built. There were no live runs: everything here used scripted agents.

5. Changed From the Design

  • Re-delivery is decided from the chain's records, not the Rollback option. A successor that dies before its first checkpoint leaves an exit that names the target, so the next Recover looks plain, and the mail would be lost.
  • A mailbox stays in ID order. The design had Transfer append, which forwarding makes wrong.
  • Artifacts are read before the bus lock, not under it, so there is no new lock order.
  • chain_delivery on run.started. The design did not foresee that a replay compares events one for one.
  • Recover refuses a retryable exit with code 0, to agree with decision 2.
  • not_restarted with cause error does not close. Decision 2 lists three causes, and the hazard says none may leave a chain open. They disagree; I followed the decision.

A child's end never closes its parent, and a parent's end never closes its child. The last row is decision 2's known limit: a re-delegation starts a new chain, and the old one stays open, with no bound on its mailbox until step 4.

7. Changed for Existing Runs

  • A send to a retryable exit now succeeds and is held. A workflow that relied on the refusal gets nothing instead, until step 3.
  • A send to a superseded PID succeeds, and its result gains forwarded_from.
  • A retryable exit keeps its subscriptions, so a publisher's count includes it.
  • An exit the engine escalated or declined can no longer be recovered by an operator.
  • In aos-tui, a line to a root that exited retryably goes in the bus, so it survives a failed turn.
  • New events: process.closed, ipc.forwarded, ipc.subscribed, ipc.unsubscribed, ipc.redelivery_failed. aos inspect shows closed.

8. Known Limits

  • B2c and B1c: no dead letters and no bounce. Step 3.
  • The no_checkpoint close is not tested through the engine. Every such exit a scripted agent can produce also returns an error, and supervise returns early on one. By reading the code, the branch may be unreachable today.
  • Resume of a live, parked process from an older checkpoint still loses mail acked after it. I read this in the code and did not measure it.
  • No aos command closes a chain; Kernel.Close is the API.
  • An exit recorded outside runSession and the engine leaves its subscription entry until the next publish to that topic drops it.
  • A re-delivered message may be acted on twice, and mail sent in rolled-back steps is not recalled. Decision 12 accepts both.
  • Replay still drops mail (decision 11 is Proposed).
