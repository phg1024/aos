Agent Indexes in the Header: Every Segment Shows Its Lines

Eval's A1 follow-up (b87b59a) found a problem with how the header showed agent indexes. It showed them newest first within 6 KB, and an older index that did not fit became a one-line pointer.

  • In the round-2 checkpoint, 4 indexes came to about 8 KB, so the oldest, art-000414, was a pointer in every draw.
  • That segment holds r2-verify-dir's answer, so that probe never tested the index.
  • Eval also found 2 of 26 indexes written on a single line ("59: … 61: … 63: …"), each kept as one entry citing only its first message.

This change shares the header's budget among all segments by whole lines, and splits run-on index lines. It is one commit on top of 50a7cfe.

Result: 2 new tests pass, 1 is updated, and the whole suite passes (go test -buildvcs=false ./internal/... ./cmd/...). RecallOff() is still byte-identical to the golden prompts. In the round-2 shape, art-000414 now shows 15 of its 24 lines. The effect on the probes is unmeasured until eval reruns.

The Rule

  • Round by round, by whole lines. Every segment with an index gets its next line before any segment gets the line after it. Within a round, the oldest segment goes first. A short index finishes early and leaves the rest of the budget to the longer ones, so unused share is redistributed with no separate step.
  • Why lines, not bytes: a line is one pointer into the archive, so an equal number of pointers per segment is the fair unit. Book suggested ⌊cap / segments⌋ bytes with redistribution. On whole lines, that gives about the same result for indexes of similar line length, and needs a second pass to place leftovers.
  • Why oldest first at the margin: the oldest segments are the ones working memory has had longest to drop. That is the opposite of the old order, which lost them first.
  • Shortened indexes say so. A shortened index ends with (+K more lines of this index: read_memory artifact_id=…). That line counts against the budget, and read_memory still returns the full index. Lines are never cut.
  • Bounded. The total shown never exceeds the budget.
  • Degenerate case. If even the "+K more" lines do not all fit, which takes about 85 segments at 6 KB, the newest segments show nothing. The trace reports all of their lines as hidden.
  • Guarantee. If the segments' first lines plus their "+K more" lines fit in the budget, every segment shows at least its first line. Round 1 adds the first lines in order, and each addition costs at most its line plus the "+K more" line already reserved.
  • Deterministic. The result depends only on the refs, in their order.

Budget and Trade-off

  • The total stays at 6 KB by default. It is now the new RecallPolicy.HeaderIndexBytes field (JSON header_index_bytes; zero means runtime.DefaultHeaderIndexBytes = 6144), so eval can hold it fixed or vary it.
  • Eval measured about +3.9K tokens per call for about 4.3 KB of index, and the header is on every call. Raising the total to show round 2 whole (about 8 KB) would add roughly another 1.8K tokens per call, for the 21 lines the fair share now leaves hidden behind "+K more".
  • With many segments the share gets thin: about 5 lines each at 12 segments of 90 B lines. Each segment's first lines still show, and the rest are one read_memory away.

Trace

memory.header is logged at a run's first call and whenever the header changes. It now records:

  • agent_index_shown.<segment> and agent_index_hidden.<segment>: what the agent saw of each index.
  • agent_index_lines_shown and agent_index_lines_hidden: the same, totalled.
  • agent_index_budget.
  • agent_index_segments: the segments showing at least one line.

Run-on Index Lines

index_memory now splits a line at each later N: or N-M: that meets all three conditions:

  • it follows whitespace;
  • it cites messages inside the archived range;
  • it is numbered after the entry before it.

So "59: a 61: b 63: c" becomes three entries. A number in an entry's text is left alone when it is below the entry's own, such as "ratio 3: 1" inside "13: …", or past the range.

Known limit: such a number is split off when it is in range and above the entry's own, such as "ratio 3: 1" inside an entry for message 1. A false split mis-cites one fragment, while a missed split loses every number after the first, so the rule leans toward splitting. The result returns the canonical index, so the agent sees the split and can resend.
